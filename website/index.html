What it is

A working week somewhere better

Your team already knows what it needs to build. What it never gets is five uninterrupted days together to do it.

Focustime is a house in the Galician countryside, far enough from anything that leaving isn’t an option. Fibre internet, so you’re fully reachable and fully productive. A separate work room with whiteboards and a wall you can cover in paper. A long table where everyone eats together.

You bring the work and the people who own it. We handle the rest — the house, every meal, the transport, the afternoon off, and everything that would otherwise land on whoever got stuck organising it.

What it isn’t

What Focustime is not

Not team building. Not a company outing. Not a retreat. And not facilitation — most teams bring their own scrum master, and it’s not our job to take that over.

It’s a working week. We just make it a very good one.

The week

How a week runs

Monday

You land in Porto, we pick you up. Lunch on arrival, then the afternoon to settle in and agree what each of you wants finished by Friday.

Tuesday

The first full day. Breakfast when you want it, lunch at the long table, dinner cooked by someone local.

Wednesday

Work until lunch. Then everyone stops: a vineyard in the Ribeira Sacra, a river, a walk. Back at the house for dinner.

Thursday

A full day, and usually the best one. By now nobody is checking their phone.

Friday

Work until eleven, then show each other what you made. Then we drive you back.

That’s three and a half working days without a single meeting invite, an interrupt from another team, or a commute. Most teams get more done than in a normal week of five.

Around that, you set your own rhythm. We keep the shape of the week; what happens inside it is yours.

Some teams fly in on Sunday. It costs one more night — around €50 per person — and turns Monday into a full working day. We’ll price it either way.

The table

Galician food, cooked by Galicians

Nobody cooks and nobody does dishes. Meals are made by local cooks with local food — not a catering company with a van.

One evening a pulpeiro arrives with a copper pot and a gas ring and cooks the octopus in front of you. Another night it’s a churrasco on the fire outside. There is empanada from the village bakery, caldo, bread from a wood oven, and Albariño from forty minutes down the road.

Tell us what people can’t eat and we’ll take care of it. That’s the whole of your involvement.

Where

Rural Galicia

Galicia is the green corner of Spain — Atlantic, forested, granite, and almost entirely unknown to the people who go to Barcelona and Alicante.

We work in the inland hills between Pontevedra and Ourense, and in the Ribeira Sacra, where the Sil river cuts canyons through terraced vineyards. Villages of thirty people. Stone houses with chestnut beams. Fibre internet, because Spain ran fibre into places you wouldn’t expect.

The coast is forty minutes away when you want it, and far enough that nobody drifts off to the beach when they shouldn’t.

How we pick

We select each house ourselves, and we don’t book anything we haven’t checked: verified fibre with real upload speed, a proper work room, exclusive use of the whole property, and nowhere within walking distance to escape to.
